CPA Improves With Time, Not Spend
Confirms the engine works

Time → CPA: r=−0.46, p<0.001, falling $0.15/month for 49 months. Spend level is not a significant CPA predictor (p=0.48).

Checkout Abandonment ≠ UX Problem
Redirects a planned redesign

Sessions/Order → Abandonment: r=0.77, p<0.001. Social/search abandonment stable. Only returning-customer (direct) traffic abandonment rose 42%→66% — an offer/pricing issue, not a site issue.

Spend → Revenue Has a 3-Month Lag
Sets evaluation timelines

Spend(t−3) → Revenue(t): r=0.945 — strongest relationship in the dataset. Don't evaluate any spend change before 90 days.

ROAS Gains Are Real, Not an Artifact
Validates current Meta strategy

Meta Spend → ROAS: r=+0.265, not significant, not negative. ROAS improvement reflects genuine creative/offer efficiency (Nicole+BOGO) — this approach scales.

2025 Acquisition Gap → 2026–27 Retention Gap
Creates urgency

New customers(t) → Returning(t+12): r=0.43, p<0.01. The 2025 acquisition collapse is already a constraint on 2026–27 returning revenue — independent of what's done now.

How to read these: "r" measures relationship strength (closer to ±1 = stronger). "p<0.001" means under 0.1% chance the pattern is random noise. "R²" measures how much of one variable's change is explained by another (0.78 = 78%).

What Happened

Outbound shipping cost was flat at $0.38/order for 32 consecutive months, then became flat at $5.12/order from Jan 2025 — an abrupt, single-month step-change (R²=0.780, p<0.0001; time-trend alone not significant, p=0.46).

Why It Matters

Shipping net P&L went from +$28,128 (2023) to −$9,117 (2025). Every other metric calculated since (ROAS, AOV, margin) already has this cost baked in — the true picture post-fix is better than it appears.

What's Next

Pull carrier invoices for Dec 2024 vs Jan 2025 (finance/ops, ~1 hour). Check for a new carrier contract, rate-card change, or disabled "calculated shipping" integration replaced with a flat-rate default.

Permanent Knowledge Base

Agency Handoff Notes

Six findings most likely to be re-discovered the hard way if undocumented. Mandatory reading for any new hire, agency, or contractor before strategy work begins.

1. CPA improves with time, not with spend level
Evidence

Time → New CPA: r=−0.46, p<0.001, β=−$0.15/month. Adspend level not a significant CPA predictor (p=0.48).

What Future Teams Should Know

Do not assume restarting spend "resets" CPA upward. The efficiency gains are durable — but only volume restores revenue.

2. BOGO is the structural offer for this business
Evidence

BOGO: 2.07× ROAS vs 1.27× no-promo on Meta. Nicole+BOGO variants: 3.0–3.44× ROAS. Confirmed across Meta, email, and discount codes.

What Future Teams Should Know

Default to BOGO as the baseline offer. Test alternatives against BOGO as the benchmark, not against "no offer."

3. The January 2025 shipping break was never investigated
Evidence

Confirmed structural break (β=−$3,010/mo, p<0.0001), not gradual drift (time-trend p=0.46, not significant). No documentation of a carrier or fulfillment change exists for this date.

What Future Teams Should Know

This remains the single highest-value unanswered question in the business. If it's somehow still unresolved when you read this, that's the first task.

4. Email volume does not drive email revenue — flows do
Evidence

Emails Sent → Net Sales: r=+0.012, not significant. One flow (Welcome) generated $153K lifetime — more than all 144 B2C campaigns combined ($102K).

What Future Teams Should Know

Prioritize flow coverage over campaign send frequency. More campaigns ≠ more revenue.

5. BWP (Amazon) is the most underanalyzed asset in the business
Evidence

BWP revenue grew 213% from 2023 to 2025 at $0 attributed CAC — but Amazon fees, returns, and fulfillment costs are not yet modeled.

What Future Teams Should Know

This could be either a hidden profit center or a margin trap depending on true fee structure. Audit before any further investment.

6. The 2025 retention deficit is already locked into 2026–27
Evidence

New customers(t) → Returning(t+12): r=0.430, p<0.01. The 2025 acquisition collapse statistically constrains 2026–27 returning revenue regardless of current actions.

What Future Teams Should Know

This is a multi-year recovery, not a quarterly fix. Set investor and stakeholder expectations accordingly — and don't panic if Q1 2026 numbers still look soft despite correct actions.
